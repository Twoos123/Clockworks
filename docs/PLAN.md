# Transpile Clyde, Reimplement Projectx, Approach Grey Havens

The 2025–2026 wave of browser ports is overwhelmingly C/C++ source (GPL releases, decompilations, leaks, reimplementations, and now static recompilation) compiled with Emscripten to WebAssembly plus WebGL2, with the player supplying their own game files into browser storage and multiplayer bridged through WebSocket gateways or WebRTC meshes; Java games have exactly one proven free route, TeaVM (the compiler behind Eaglercraft's browser Minecraft), and one commercial "run the unmodified JAR" route, CheerpJ. Spiral Knights is a hard case for both. Its engine layers (Clyde, Narya, Nenya, Getdown) are open source and are being actively modernized to **Java 25 and LWJGL 3.4.3** by a Grey Havens engineer as of October 2026, but every piece of game logic lives in an obfuscated **projectx-pcode.jar that is re-obfuscated on every patch**, the renderer is a fixed-function OpenGL compatibility-profile design, the client still needs AWT at runtime, and the game is a live, monetized service shipping near-weekly releases whose Terms of Service ban decompiling, deciphering transmissions, and third-party servers. The closest analogue, Sega's PSO Reborn browser port, was DMCA'd **ten days after multiplayer went live** in September 2026. The plan this report recommends is therefore a clean-room browser client built on the BSD/LGPL Clyde/Narya stack through TeaVM (JavaScript and Wasm GC builds), a WebGL2 fixed-function emulation layer, bring-your-own-assets loaded from the player's own install into OPFS, zero projectx bytes ever served, and no connection to Grey Havens' servers unless they authorize it, pursued alongside an early, low-key approach to Grey Havens that offers them the browser Clyde runtime as something they can adopt. The first proof of concept is a browser Clyde model viewer that renders an animated model from the user's own rsrc folder, because it exercises the three riskiest subsystems (binary deserialization under a closed-world compiler, fixed-function GL emulation, AWT replacement) without touching proprietary code or live servers. Budget for a multi-year effort: months to the viewer, a year or more to an offline playable slice, and live-service play only with Grey Havens' blessing.

## Emscripten, WebGL2 and bring-your-own-files define the 2026 port wave

Two independent indexes now track the phenomenon the question refers to. Recompiledgames.com lists **52 browser or decompilation projects**, tags each one "Instant" or "Own files" for asset status, and warns that projects which include or stream assets or rebuild current commercial games carry the most legal risk; the awesome-decomp-games list keeps a "Play in your browser" section of **41 entries**, re-checks links daily in a real browser, and hides the ones that get taken down ([Recompiled Games](https://recompiledgames.com/); [awesome-decomp-games](https://github.com/SubmitGame/awesome-decomp-games)). The raw material comes in five kinds: official source releases (id Tech 2 and 3, EA's 2025 GPL-3 release of Command & Conquer Generals), reverse-engineered or decompiled source (re3/reVC for GTA, Devilution for Diablo, the n64decomp projects behind Mario 64 and Ocarina of Time, the Xbox Halo decompilation), leaked source (Half-Life 2's Source lineage, GunZ, The Simpsons: Hit & Run), clean reimplementations (Xash3D for GoldSrc, OpenRCT2, OpenTTD, VCMI, OpenBW), and most recently static binary recompilation, where PS2 and PSX executables are translated ahead of time into C++ and then to Wasm, with the graphics synthesizer emulated in WebGPU compute shaders ([Recompiled Games](https://recompiledgames.com/); [HN 50052251](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50052251)). Nearly all of it is C or C++, which is why nearly all of it goes through the same pipeline.

That pipeline is now a recipe. Make sure the engine has an SDL2 or SDL3 platform layer, compile with Emscripten using its SDL/zlib/libpng ports, map the original graphics API onto WebGL2 through a shim, restructure the blocking main loop, put user-supplied data into OPFS or IDBFS and saves into IndexedDB, serve the result statically with cross-origin-isolation headers, and bridge networking through WebSocket gateways or WebRTC. The shim is dictated by the original API: Pieter Levels' Return to Castle Wolfenstein port runs the engine's OpenGL 1.x through **GL4ES onto WebGL2**, the GunZ and Generals ports keep their Direct3D 9 and 8 calls intact and translate them at runtime through LostMyCode's d3d9-webgl layer and its d3d8 derivative, and the N64 decomps reuse libultraship's fast3d renderer ([levels.io](https://levels.io/ported-return-to-castle-wolfenstein-to-web-with-ai); [dev.to GunZ port](https://dev.to/whiplash/how-i-fully-ported-a-heavily-windows-dependent-online-game-to-the-browser-with-webassembly-1fea); [Generals-WebAssembly](https://github.com/caiiiycuk/Generals-WebAssembly); [perfect_dark_web](https://github.com/moeiscool/perfect_dark_web)). Emscripten itself ships a WebGL-friendly ES2/ES3 subset by default, slower emulation modes for client-side arrays and buffer mapping, and a desktop-GL legacy emulation it describes as "by no means complete," and it names Regal and gl4es as the options for genuinely fixed-function code ([Emscripten OpenGL support](https://emscripten.org/docs/porting/multimedia_and_graphics/OpenGL-support.html)). Asset handling has converged on bring-your-own-files because that is where the legal line has actually been enforced. Hosted "instant play" sites that serve game data (the pieter.com family, csjs.live) are the ones absorbing exposure; GitHub-hosted projects almost universally take a folder or archive from the user, and both Mario 64 browser projects that drew press attention retreated to requiring the player's own ROM after takedown pressure ([XDA](https://www.xda-developers.com/you-can-now-play-super-mario-64-in-browser-with-12-player-multiplayer/)). One author went a step further and regenerated every ROM-derived texture, font and sound sample as CC0 for Mario 64, Ocarina of Time and Halo so that no original files are needed at all ([sm64-cleanroom](https://github.com/andrewnakas/sm64-cleanroom)). The browser-side mechanics are now standard: a directory picker (Chromium only) or drag-and-drop, extraction in a Web Worker, a content store in OPFS using synchronous access handles, IndexedDB for saves, and a Service Worker that answers fetches from local storage for offline play ([MDN OPFS](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/File_System_API/Origin_private_file_system); [Chrome Developers: File System Access](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/capabilities/web-apis/file-system-access)).

Networking is where the browser's lack of raw sockets forces an architectural choice every time. The Ocarina port simply disabled its socket-dependent features; q3js fronts real Quake III dedicated servers with a WebSocket-to-UDP gateway; Generals bridges the engine's UDP into a WebRTC mesh where the host relays; Halo browsers play each other in WebRTC rooms with no project server at all; GunZ ran the game server inside the tab and talked to a real one over WebSocket; Celeste tunneled its TCP through a Wisp proxy ([Shipwright PR 2](https://github.com/Project516/Shipwright/pull/2); [lklacar/q3js](https://github.com/lklacar/q3js); [Generals-WebAssembly](https://github.com/caiiiycuk/Generals-WebAssembly); [OpenCE](https://github.com/OpenCommunityEdition/OpenCE); [dev.to GunZ port](https://dev.to/whiplash/how-i-fully-ported-a-heavily-windows-dependent-online-game-to-the-browser-with-webassembly-1fea); [velzie blog](https://velzie.rip/blog/celeste-wasm)). Eaglercraft's answer for a TCP MMO-style protocol, a server-side plugin that terminates wss and speaks the native protocol onward, is the template that matters for Spiral Knights and is discussed in the next section.

Hosting and threading are, in practice, one decision. Emscripten pthreads need SharedArrayBuffer, which requires the COOP and COEP headers, and GitHub Pages cannot set response headers; the common workaround is the coi-serviceworker shim, which injects the headers and reloads the page once ([Emscripten pthreads](https://emscripten.org/docs/porting/pthreads.html); [GitHub Community #13309](https://github.com/orgs/community/discussions/13309); [coi-serviceworker](https://github.com/gzuidhof/coi-serviceworker)). Cloudflare Pages supports a headers file; itch.io caps HTML5 games at **500 MB extracted and 200 MB per file**; Steam takes no URL, so browser games ship there inside Electron or NW.js ([Cloudflare Pages headers](https://developers.cloudflare.com/pages/configuration/headers/); [itch.io HTML5 docs](https://itch.io/docs/creators/html5); [Summer Engine](https://www.summerengine.com/make-great-games/threejs-game-on-steam)). The Terraria porter found Cloudflare Pages' 25 MB file limit unusable for 100 MB+ wasm files and Firebase exhausted its bandwidth quota, and fell back to GitHub Pages ([HN 44101175](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44101175)).

What breaks is remarkably consistent. The Ship of Harkinian pull request is the most candid public record: Asyncify instrumentation caused one function to reach **53,185 locals and the link step to take two to three hours** until inlining was capped, randomizer tables had to be compiled unoptimized because optimization needed more than 4 GB of RAM per file, a 16-thread machine hung the resource loader until it was limited to one or two threads, and a full CI job takes about 53 minutes ([Shipwright PR 2](https://github.com/Project516/Shipwright/pull/2)). Asyncify, the default way to keep a blocking game loop, costs "something like 50%" at runtime and inflates code; JSPI removes that overhead but Emscripten's own main-loop helpers were reported incompatible with it ([Emscripten Asyncify](https://emscripten.org/docs/porting/asyncify.html); [emscripten issue 22493](https://github.com/emscripten-core/emscripten/issues/22493)). Wasm32 is capped at 4 GB; Memory64 shipped in Chrome 133 and Firefox 134 but costs roughly 10% to 100% in load/store performance and remains flag-only in Safari, so no port in this survey ships it ([SpiderMonkey blog](https://spidermonkey.dev/blog/2025/01/15/is-memory64-actually-worth-using.html)). Frame pacing tied to requestAnimationFrame ran Celeste Classic at double speed on 120 Hz displays, and Perfect Dark misbehaves above ~165 FPS ([HN 44101175](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44101175); [perfect_dark_web](https://github.com/moeiscool/perfect_dark_web)). Godot, which has to ship to the widest audience of anyone, reintroduced single-threaded web builds in 4.3 precisely because isolation headers were hard to obtain on itch.io and ad-supported portals and SharedArrayBuffer froze browsers on macOS and iOS ([Godot 4.3 progress report](https://godotengine.org/article/progress-report-web-export-in-4-3/)). And the legal breakage is real: Take-Two's agent objected even to DOS.Zone inviting users to supply keys from their own Vice City copies, Activision DMCA'd the vibe-coded Black Ops 2 recreations in October 2026, and Kotaku counted "hundreds" of AI-decompiled browser games appearing in a couple of weeks while decomp developers called the trend a race to the bottom ([Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/video-games/browser-run-copy-of-grand-theft-auto-gets-taken-down-by-dcma-take-two-says-dos-zone-infringed-companys-intellectual-property-rights-despite-disclaimers-and-requirement-to-own-original-copy-of-title-to-run-full-game-online); [XDA](https://www.xda-developers.com/activision-hits-vibe-coded-classic-call-of-duty-browser-ports-with-dmca-takedowns/); [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/we-might-be-cooked-as-these-vibe-coded-web-browser-ports-of-halo-the-simpsons-hit-and-run-and-gta-vice-city-seem-to-work-perfectly-2000743300)).

The precedents that bracket an MMO specifically are Flyff Universe and RuneScape. Gala Lab's 2022 HTML5 relaunch of a 2005-era 3D MMO runs on PC, Mac and mobile with no download, passed two million registrations, and returned Gala to operating profit, though no technical write-up exists and the credit to an "all-new graphics engine" suggests a renderer rewrite rather than a transpile ([MMOs.com](https://mmos.com/news/flyff-universe-global-cross-platform-relaunch); [gamebiz](https://gamebiz.jp/news/364251)). Jagex's 2013 HTML5 RuneScape client never left beta on that browser generation and was replaced by a native client in 2016 ([RuneScape Wiki](https://runescape.wiki/w/HTML5)). The difference between the two is WebAssembly, WebGL2 and ubiquitous mobile GPUs, which is to say a 2026 port starts from a far stronger baseline than either.

## Java has two browser routes, and Eaglercraft already proved one

None of the Emscripten machinery applies to bytecode, so a Java game has two real options and several non-options. **TeaVM** is an ahead-of-time compiler from Java bytecode (not source, so javac, kotlinc and scalac output all work) to JavaScript or Wasm GC; it deliberately restricts reflection, resources, class loaders and JNI, emulates threads as green threads through a continuation-passing transformation, and tells "tightly coupled Swing applications" to look elsewhere ([TeaVM overview](https://teavm.org/docs/intro/overview.html)). It is healthy: **0.16.0 shipped on 2 October 2026**, roughly the second feature release of the year, and the recent history is directly relevant to a game port, with the Wasm GC backend arriving in 0.11.0, reaching near parity with the JS backend in 0.12.0, gaining coroutine-based threads and Java 25 support in 0.13.0, and 0.14.0 deleting the old linear-memory Wasm and WASI backends entirely; 0.16.0 added WebGPU bindings ([TeaVM 0.16.0](https://github.com/konsoletyper/teavm/releases/tag/0.16.0); [TeaVM releases](https://github.com/konsoletyper/teavm/releases)). The Wasm GC backend is still shaking out codegen bugs, as a try/finally miscompile filed against 0.16.0 two days after release shows ([teavm issue #1274](https://github.com/konsoletyper/teavm/issues/1274)).

TeaVM's constraints map precisely onto what Clyde and Narya do. Its class-library report shows **java.nio.channels at 15% coverage, java.net at 12%, java.util.concurrent at 14%, java.security at 8%**, and java.awt, java.awt.image, javax.imageio and javax.sound absent altogether, which means Narya's SocketChannel-based transport, any executor-heavy code, the RSA/AES handshake, and every AWT image or font path must be replaced rather than compiled ([TeaVM JCL report](https://teavm.org/jcl-report/recent/jcl.html)). Blocking I/O is modeled with an asynchronous-native pattern that lets a "thread" suspend on a WebSocket message or a fetch, but a coroutine cannot suspend inside a raw JavaScript callback such as requestAnimationFrame, and 0.16.0 now throws a clear exception when that happens, so the render loop must be structured so the frame callback never blocks ([TeaVM coroutines doc](https://www.teavm.org/docs/runtime/coroutines.html); [TeaVM 0.16.0](https://github.com/konsoletyper/teavm/releases/tag/0.16.0)). Reflection is whitelist-only: classes must be granted by a reflection policy and separately preserved from dead-code elimination, with no package scanning ([gdx-teavm usage.md](https://github.com/xpenatan/gdx-teavm/blob/master/docs/usage.md)). The Wasm GC loader keeps direct NIO buffers in a linear memory whose maximum defaults to 2 GiB and can link Emscripten-built C modules into the same memory, which is how native decoders could be brought along ([Wasm GC loader API](https://www.teavm.org/docs/wasm-gc-backend/loader.html)).

**Eaglercraft** is the existence proof that a full LWJGL-era Java 3D game runs on TeaVM. Its developer rewrote LWJGL by hand, a task reported as taking about a month, wrote an "OpenGL 1.3 emulator built on top of WebGL 2.0" with a WebGL 1 fallback, proxied Minecraft's TCP protocol over WebSockets through a server-side plugin (EaglerXBungee, now EaglerXServer for Spigot, BungeeCord and Velocity), added WebRTC "Shared Worlds" with relay-based signaling, shipped assets as a single bundle, kept a desktop LWJGL3 runtime purely because debugging crashes on the JVM is faster than waiting for browser builds, and in 2024–2025 added an experimental WASM-GC build on a custom TeaVM fork that is "around 50% more FPS and TPS in some cases" but depends on JSPI ([HandWiki](https://handwiki.org/wiki/Software:Eaglercraft); [Lingpand/webmc README](https://github.com/Lingpand/webmc); [Modrinth EaglercraftXServer](https://modrinth.com/mod/eaglercraftxserver); [EaglercraftX README mirror](https://github.com/3kh0/eaglercraft-builds/blob/main/EaglercraftX_1.8_README.md); [eagler-teavm](https://github.com/Eaglercraft-TeaVM-Fork/eagler-teavm)). The legal history is the other half of the lesson: Mojang had **92 repositories removed in February 2023**, its notice led with the MINECRAFT trademark, fifteen copyright registrations and a §1201 anti-circumvention theory that GitHub declined while honoring the copyright claims, enforcement spread to GitLab, Discord and Google search, and the GitHub mirrors return HTTP 451 today ([TorrentFreak](https://torrentfreak.com/mojang-targets-repositories-of-browser-based-minecraft-copy-eaglercraft-230224/); [github/dmca 2023-03-13](https://github.com/github/dmca/blob/master/2023/03/2023-03-13-mojang-2.md)). The toolchain was never the target; distributing decompiled Mojang code and assets was.

Two smaller TeaVM precedents fill in the engine-porting details. **gdx-teavm** is the libGDX backend on TeaVM with both JavaScript and Wasm targets; its 1.6.2 release (24 August 2026, on TeaVM 0.15.0) made GL30 the default with automatic WebGL1 fallback, and five releases between February and August 2026 show the WebGL-over-JSO path is exercised by shipping games ([gdx-teavm releases](https://github.com/xpenatan/gdx-teavm/releases)). The 2023 jMonkeyEngine web port is the closest thing to a Clyde-sized engine on TeaVM: rendering went to WebGL 2, audio to Web Audio with canned reverb, physics to an Emscripten Bullet build, networking was left unsupported pending a WebSocket connector, AWT dialogs were unsupported, and the nastiest rendering bug was an RGB8-to-RGB565 format mapping that desktop drivers silently upgraded but mobile and AMD GPUs exposed ([jME TeaVM port thread](https://hub.jmonkeyengine.org/t/porting-jmonkeyengine-to-the-browser-with-webgl2-and-teavm-sourcecode-released/47020)).

**CheerpJ** is the opposite philosophy: a full OpenJDK compiled from C++ to WebAssembly, with an interpreter that profiles cold code and a JIT that compiles hot bytecode to JavaScript, running unmodified JARs including obfuscated ones, rendering AWT and Swing windows as HTML elements and canvases, mapping same-origin HTTP to fetch, and offering general networking only through Tailscale ([CheerpJ architecture](https://cheerpj.com/docs/explanation/architecture)). Version 4.0 introduced WebAssembly JNI modules loaded dynamically like shared libraries, and the showcase that matters here, Browsercraft, runs the **unmodified Minecraft 1.2.5 client.jar with unmodified LWJGL JNI natives compiled as a CheerpJ Wasm module and gl4es bridging OpenGL to WebGL**, the vendor describing Minecraft as a stress test that now runs with "satisfactory performance on most mid range machines" ([CheerpJ 4.0 blog](https://labs.leaningtech.com/blog/cheerpj-4.0); [Browsercraft showcase](https://labs.leaningtech.com/showcase/browsercraft); [CheerpJ 4.1 blog](https://labs.leaningtech.com/blog/cheerpj-4.1)). The catches are material. The JNI toolchain for user-built modules was "planned for general release" in 4.0 and 4.1 and no announcement of its availability was found; 4.3 (21 April 2026) supports Java 8, 11 and 17-preview with Java 21 still "planned for later this year," behind the original roadmap; the Community tier is free only for individuals and one-person companies and excludes self-hosting the runtime, with Small Business at **£100 per developer per month**; and the FAQ states flatly that browsers provide no raw sockets, so RuneScape does not work ([CheerpJ 4.3 blog](https://labs.leaningtech.com/blog/cheerpj-4.3); [CheerpJ licensing](https://cheerpj.com/licensing/); [CheerpJ FAQ](https://cheerpj.com/docs/faq)). No CheerpJ 3 or 4 document describing how java.lang.Thread is scheduled was found, which matters for a client with loader and network threads.

The non-options are quickly dismissed. GraalVM Web Image (Oracle GraalVM 25.1+, Wasm GC output) is officially experimental, and a secondary report describes multithreading as hampered and a mid-size demo compiling to 41 MB; J2CL's Wasm backend is experimental, Bazel-only and source-based; Kotlin/Wasm is Beta and does not consume Java bytecode; JWebAssembly lacks exceptions, threads and reflection; Bytecoder is stuck at Java 8–20 and its libGDX backend is archived with a pointer to gdx-teavm ([GraalVM Web Image docs](https://www.graalvm.org/dev/reference-manual/web-image/); [Kitemetric](https://kitemetric.com/blogs/graalvm-wasm-a-new-dawn-for-j2cl-gwt-developers); [J2CL](https://github.com/google/j2cl/blob/master/docs/getting-started-j2wasm.md); [Kotlin/Wasm overview](https://kotlinlang.org/docs/wasm-overview.html); [JWebAssembly](https://github.com/i-net-software/JWebAssembly); [Bytecoder](https://github.com/mirkosertic/Bytecoder); [gdx-backend-bytecoder](https://github.com/squins/gdx-backend-bytecoder/)). GraalVM Web Image is the one worth re-checking in a year or two because its closed-world reflection configuration is the same discipline a TeaVM port imposes anyway.

|---|---|---|

## Spiral Knights is half open-source, half obfuscated, and a live service with teeth

The engine side is in better shape than a 2011 game has any right to be. Clyde, Three Rings' BSD-licensed 3D game library, provides the renderer, scene graph and compositor, materials, static and articulated models with procedural animation, particles, a JME-BUI-derived GUI, the versioned XML/binary export system, the reflective config and editor system, and the Tudey 2.5D engine "with 2D server logic and 3D graphics" ([Clyde README](https://github.com/threerings/clyde)). Its current master targets **Java 25** and depends on **LWJGL 3.4.3 with lwjgl-opengl, lwjgl-openal, lwjgl-glfw and the lwjgl3-awt bridge**, with natives for Linux, Windows, macOS and macOS-arm64 and no JOGL, JInput or jME dependencies; the newest commit is dated **7 October 2026** and the last fifteen are all by raygreenwell, whose GitHub profile reads "Creative multiplayer game builder at Grey Havens" and several of whose commit messages reference AI-assisted cleanup ([clyde pom.xml](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/threerings/clyde/master/pom.xml); [clyde core/pom.xml](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/threerings/clyde/master/core/pom.xml); [clyde commits](https://github.com/threerings/clyde/commits/master); [raygreenwell](https://github.com/raygreenwell)). Anything describing Spiral Knights as a Java 6–8 / LWJGL 2 game is pre-2026 and should be read as history.

The renderer is the central technical problem. Clyde's Renderer class makes heavy use of the fixed-function pipeline: the matrix stack, lights and materials, fog, texture environments and texgen, client-side vertex arrays, alpha test, user clip planes, color sum and display-list deletion, while also using GL20 shaders and vertex attributes, GL15 VBOs and occlusion queries, GL30 VAOs (with a comment that macOS Metal-backed GL requires them even in compatibility profile) and framebuffers, GL31 rectangle textures and GL43 debug output, all through LWJGL 3's capability API; the file's copyright header still ends in 2012 and its comments reference old OS X, ATI and Intel driver workarounds ([Renderer.java](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/threerings/clyde/master/core/src/main/java/com/threerings/opengl/renderer/Renderer.java)). WebGL2 has none of the fixed-function state. The Android port, SKapsule, solves this by running the real client on **JRE 25 and LWJGL 3.4.1 with gl4es translating OpenGL to GLES, openal-soft, caciocavallo as a "headless AWT bridge," and Getdown pulling the game from Grey Havens' official servers**, and reports that the game "boots, logs in, and is playable, but has rough edges" ([SKonstruct/SKapsule](https://github.com/SKonstruct/SKapsule)). The need for an AWT bridge even on Android is the strongest public evidence that the client needs java.awt classes at runtime, not just in the Swing editors, and the presence of both GlDisplayApp (GLFW) and GlCanvasApp (AWT canvas) in Clyde means two windowing paths exist, with no public statement of which the live client uses.

The live client itself was overhauled in 2026. Grey Havens' CEO wrote in December 2025 that the main project was "modernizing the Java version" to reduce server-side GC lag, after which they would "upgrade [LWJGL] for further performance gains," with "5/6 of the Three Rings founding team on the case"; the Java update shipped **18 February 2026** as "a major update to modernize clients which will enable us to do a lot of things we otherwise cannot," a Game Master later confirmed the client now bundles its own runtime, and the **5 May 2026** "System Upgrade" added Apple-silicon support and new macOS window handling ([Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/99900/discussions/0/685240596077506930/); [forum node/125065](https://forums.spiralknights.com/en/node/125065); [forum node/125248](https://forums.spiralknights.com/en/node/125248); [wiki Release/all](https://wiki.spiralknights.com/Release/all)). Wikipedia states the bundled runtime is Java 21, Clyde master targets 25, and SKapsule bundles 25; no primary source pins the number, and no official note says "LWJGL 3" explicitly, though the Clyde dependency change, the CEO's roadmap and the Apple-silicon release together make it very likely ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spiral_Knights)).

Networking is Narya's Presents layer, LGPL-2.1, and its wire behavior is fully readable in source. The client opens a blocking SocketChannel to the first configured port, writes **4-byte big-endian length-prefixed frames (the length includes the prefix)** containing objects serialized by Three Rings' own ObjectOutputStream, performs an optional secure handshake in which it sends its public-key credentials, receives a SecureResponse, and then sends an AES-wrapped auth request whose session secret also authenticates the optional UDP channel, waits for a bootstrap notification, and only then starts the datagram reader, pinging each datagram port up to ten times and falling back to TCP-only if none answers; the class does no automatic reconnect ([BlockingCommunicator.java](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/threerings/narya/master/core/src/main/java/com/threerings/presents/client/BlockingCommunicator.java); [FramingOutputStream.java](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/threerings/narya/master/core/src/main/java/com/threerings/io/FramingOutputStream.java)). Spiral Knights specifically listens on **TCP 47624 and UDP 47625**, and the server's RSA public key, host and ports live in deployment.properties inside the client's config.jar, according to the README of Thetya, the one public server reimplementation, whose main class extends Clyde's TudeyServer and sets enum serialization to ordinal ([excal69/thetya](https://github.com/excal69/thetya); [ThetyaServer.java](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/excal69/thetya/main/src/com/github/thetya/server/ThetyaServer.java)). A community packet sniffer confirms the port and shows plaintext Java class names and resource paths in the payloads ([live_monitor.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/xiao2718/Spiral-Knights-Traffic-Monitor/main/live_monitor.py)). Narya's own parent POM targets Java 21 and notes that "projectx builds with -Djavac.lint=none," a small reminder that the open repos are maintained for the live game ([narya pom.xml](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/threerings/narya/master/pom.xml)).

Assets are Clyde export files. The BinaryExporter writes a magic number of **0xFACEAF0E**, a version short, a flag word, and then a Deflate stream (not GZIP) of varint-identified objects in which classes are written by name with flag bytes and Exportable objects carry per-class field-id tables, so a reader needs an inflate step, the varint and table decoder, and the field schema of every class that appears ([BinaryExporter.java](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/threerings/clyde/master/core/src/main/java/com/threerings/export/BinaryExporter.java)). The rsrc tree holds config catalogs under rsrc/config (item.dat, actor.dat, accessory.dat, attack.dat, shader.dat, texture.dat, wall.dat, level_table.dat and about fifty others), model files under paths like rsrc/character/npc, PNG textures alongside them, OGG/MP3/WAV audio played through Nenya's OpenAL layer, and Tudey scenes that the client downloads from the server only as needed and caches in a scenes folder ([xiao2718/sk-stat-extractor](https://github.com/xiao2718/sk-stat-extractor); [forum node/113148](https://forums.spiralknights.com/en/node/113148); [nenya core/pom.xml](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/threerings/nenya/master/core/pom.xml); [SpiralKnightsSceneArchive](https://github.com/sinkillerj/SpiralKnightsSceneArchive)). Community tooling is mature and instructive: SpiralView rebuilds Clyde's editors against the game, ThreeRingsSharp is an IKVM port of Clyde/Narya/Nenya/Vilya that rips models to glTF, Kozmadeus imports OBJ/DAE into Clyde model XML, and Clyde itself still ships an xml2dat tool that was touched on 3 October 2026 ([lucasluqui/spiralview](https://github.com/lucasluqui/spiralview); [EtiTheSpirit/ThreeRingsSharp](https://github.com/EtiTheSpirit/ThreeRingsSharp); [Crowfunder/Kozmadeus](https://github.com/Crowfunder/Kozmadeus); [clyde commits](https://github.com/threerings/clyde/commits/master)). The crucial caveat is that every tool that reads game config catalogs requires projectx-pcode.jar on the classpath, because the catalog objects are instances of game-specific classes; model, material and animation files are Clyde classes and are the exception a port can start from.

The proprietary layer is where everything gets hard. The game code is code/projectx-pcode.jar plus projectx-config.jar and config.jar, and modders document that **"each game update reobfuscates classes, fields, and methods,"** so names shift between patches and class-replacement mods locate targets by structural signature rather than name; Clyde's dependency on proguard-annotations is consistent with a ProGuard build ([xiao2718/spiral-knights-modutils](https://github.com/xiao2718/spiral-knights-modutils); [clyde core/pom.xml](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/threerings/clyde/master/core/pom.xml)). Public reverse engineering is thin: Thetya has one commit, no stars, no license and no stated status, with a 16 MB "early source" zip on the Internet Archive from October 2023; a headless client logs into live servers but supports only read-only exchange-market events; and Knight Launcher, the GPL-3 launcher and mod manager with 90 stars, states in its README that "third party server support will not be enabled while Grey Havens' Official servers are still online, unless explicitly permitted by their respective right owners and/or law" ([excal69/thetya](https://github.com/excal69/thetya); [archive.org](https://archive.org/details/thetya-spiral-knights); [azzerial/spiral-knights-headless-client](https://github.com/azzerial/spiral-knights-headless-client); [lucasluqui/KnightLauncher](https://github.com/lucasluqui/KnightLauncher)).

The legal reality follows from the game being alive. Grey Havens, an LLC of former Three Rings staff with 11–50 employees on LinkedIn, shipped the Coliseum Crucible level editor in November 2025, Super Brawl PvP in December, the Java modernization in February 2026, the System Upgrade in May, and releases on 8 July, 17 July, 30 July, 5 August, 26 August, 9 September, 16 September ("Blade Serenade") and 5 October ("How Bizarre"), while continuing to sell prize boxes ([LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/company/grey-havens-llc); [wiki Release/all](https://wiki.spiralknights.com/Release/all); [MMOHuts](https://mmohuts.com/news/spiral-knights-gets-a-rare-major-update-with-new-pvp-player-made-arenas-and-more-servers)). The Terms of Service, contracting with Grey Havens, LLC under California law with San Francisco venue, prohibit in **§6.1** reverse engineering, decompiling or disassembling the game "or any portion thereof" including derived utilities and emulators, in **§6.2** providing any other means by which the game may be played, with server emulators as the example, in **§6.13** modifying files, disassembling the client or deciphering data transmissions on pain of immediate termination and possible civil or criminal liability, and in **§6.15** playing on any server not provided or authorized by Grey Havens ([SK ToS](https://www.spiralknights.com/tos.xhtml)). The one public enforcement episode is the April 2021 forum thread titled "DMCA Take Down," in which a staff account stated that redistributing the client or media assets, facilitating cheats or mods, or creating a private server violate the ToS and the law, that Grey Havens is "often willing to work with players who add to the game, report issues, or create tools, provided its legal rights are honored," and that it acted because an individual announced a private server during the anniversary weekend; the targeted developer was the Knight Launcher author, who was banned ([SK forums node/124035](https://forums.spiralknights.com/en/node/124035)).

The wider 2025–2026 pattern says live-service rights holders are escalating. Sega's enforcement vendor took down PSO Reborn, a browser port of Phantasy Star Online whose multiplayer went live on 14 September 2026, with the takedown reported on 24 September ([VGC](https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/phantasy-star-online-is-now-fully-playable-in-your-web-browser-via-pso-reborn/); [Power Up Gaming](https://powerupgaming.co.uk/2026/09/24/phantasy-star-browser-port-pso-reborn-hit-with-sega-dmca-notice/)). Blizzard obtained a permanent injunction against Turtle WoW in April 2026 that covers modded and "remastered" clients, not just servers, after Turtle WoW had asked for a licensing framework; Daybreak's suit against The Heroes' Journey frames required client modifications as DMCA circumvention ([PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/world-of-warcraft/turtle-wow-cease-and-desist); [Aftermath](https://aftermath.site/everquest-the-heroes-journey-lawsuit-update/)). The licensed fan projects that exist, Homecoming for City of Heroes and Project 1999 for EverQuest, share a profile Spiral Knights does not fit: a sunset official game, donation-only funding, a legal entity, four-plus years of quiet cooperation, and acceptance of the publisher's conditions and kill switch ([Homecoming announcement](https://forums.homecomingservers.com/topic/47223-ncsoft-homecoming-license-announcement/); [everquest.com](https://www.everquest.com/news/project-1999-daybreak)). On the other side of the ledger, the Ninth Circuit, which would hear any dispute under the ToS venue clause, holds in Sega v. Accolade and Sony v. Connectix that intermediate copying for interoperability can be fair use where the final product contains none of the plaintiff's code ([Copyright Office summary of Connectix](https://www.copyright.gov/fair-use/summaries/sony-connectix-9thcir2000.pdf)). And the open-source licenses cut cleanly: Clyde and Getdown are BSD-2-Clause, requiring only notice retention, while Narya, Nenya, Vilya and samskivert are LGPL-2.1, so any browser modifications to those libraries must be published under LGPL when distributed, and because a whole-program transpile into one bundle raises an unresolved LGPL §6 relinking question, the conservative answer is to publish the entire client source, which the recommended plan does anyway ([clyde LICENSE](https://github.com/threerings/clyde/blob/master/LICENSE); [getdown LICENSE](https://github.com/threerings/getdown/blob/master/LICENSE); [Narya README](https://github.com/threerings/narya); [threerings/nenya](https://github.com/threerings/nenya); [threerings/vilya](https://github.com/threerings/vilya)).

| Spiral Knights component | Status | What it means in a browser |
|---|---|---|
| Clyde (renderer, models, Tudey, export, GUI) | BSD, Java 25, LWJGL 3.4.3, actively maintained | Portable through TeaVM with a new platform layer; fixed-function renderer needs a WebGL2 emulator |
| Narya Presents protocol | LGPL-2.1, blocking TCP, 4-byte frames, RSA/AES handshake, optional UDP | Transport must become WebSocket; frames map 1:1; crypto via Web Crypto; UDP skipped |

## Four strategic options, and why clean-room plus courtship wins

The decision is less about compilers than about what bytes the website serves and whose servers the client talks to. Those two variables predict every outcome in the precedent set: Eaglercraft, the compiled Mario 64 PC builds, PSO Reborn, Nostalrius, Turtle WoW and The Heroes' Journey each shipped the rights holder's code or assets or competed with a live service, while OpenMW, OpenRCT2, DevilutionX, Ship of Harkinian, pokeemerald and Return of Reckoning shipped only their own code against user-supplied data and were left alone or eventually licensed ([OpenMW FAQ](https://openmw.org/faq/); [Wikipedia: Ship of Harkinian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ship_of_Harkinian); [VGC](https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/nintendo-takes-action-against-mario-64-pc-port/)). Four options present themselves.

**Option A, transpile projectx-pcode.jar through TeaVM in the Eaglercraft pattern**, is the fastest route to something recognizably playable: the engine is already Java, TeaVM consumes bytecode, and the work is the platform layer plus reflection whitelists. It is also the worst option. The site would be serving Grey Havens' copyrighted game code in transpiled form, which is exactly what Mojang attacked and GitHub honored as a copyright claim; the developer would breach ToS §6.1 and §6.13 by decompiling and tracing the obfuscated classes; the re-obfuscation on every patch means the whitelist and shim work partially resets every few weeks; and the only thing such a client is good for is connecting to Grey Havens' servers, which is §6.15 and the behavior currently being litigated as circumvention ([github/dmca 2023-03-13](https://github.com/github/dmca/blob/master/2023/03/2023-03-13-mojang-2.md); [xiao2718/spiral-knights-modutils](https://github.com/xiao2718/spiral-knights-modutils); [SK ToS](https://www.spiralknights.com/tos.xhtml)). The 2021 episode shows Grey Havens responds to exactly this kind of surprise with bans and takedown language, and a company whose revenue depends on one live game has every incentive to act fast ([SK forums node/124035](https://forums.spiralknights.com/en/node/124035)).

**Option B, run the player's own projectx jar inside CheerpJ from their install directory**, is the most legally interesting and the least proven. Nothing of Grey Havens' would be served; the site would host only the CheerpJ runtime, a loader, and whatever LWJGL/gl4es module the vendor can supply, and the player's own code/ folder would be loaded at runtime from disk, the way the Celeste web port loads the user's own executable ([velzie blog](https://velzie.rip/blog/celeste-wasm)). AWT would simply work. But the LWJGL-as-Wasm-JNI toolchain is vendor-only today, Java 21 and 25 class files are not supported, CheerpJ's thread model is undocumented, startup is interpreter-first, the license is commercial above one-person scale and forbids self-hosting the runtime on the free tier, and the game would still have no server to talk to ([CheerpJ 4.3 blog](https://labs.leaningtech.com/blog/cheerpj-4.3); [CheerpJ licensing](https://cheerpj.com/licensing/)). No project anywhere has executed a proprietary Java client from the user's disk in a browser, and the developer still has to reverse-engineer enough of the obfuscated jar to make the launch path work. Treat this as a research spike worth a week, not a plan.

**Option C, a clean-room client on the open Clyde/Narya stack with bring-your-own-assets and no live-server connectivity**, is the longest road and the only one with a defensible posture. The site serves BSD and LGPL code plus code you wrote; the player's rsrc and scenes folders are read from their own legally obtained install and never leave their machine; the Presents transport is reimplemented from open LGPL source rather than from captured traffic; and game-layer logic is reimplemented against the observable formats, the same position Ship of Harkinian and OpenMW occupy and the one the Accolade/Connectix line protects best ([Copyright Office summary of Connectix](https://www.copyright.gov/fair-use/summaries/sony-connectix-9thcir2000.pdf)). The cost is that Spiral Knights' game logic, item systems, mission structure, combat rules and UI all live in projectx and would have to be rebuilt, and that an offline or community-server slice is what results, not the live game. The engineering is front-loaded in the parts that are reusable regardless (the Clyde web runtime), and every intermediate deliverable, a model viewer, a scene viewer, an offline Crucible arena, is a tool of the kind Grey Havens said it tolerates.

**Option D, an official or blessed port with Grey Havens**, is the only path to live-service play in a browser, and the circumstances are unusually favorable for a small studio. The team is the original Three Rings founders, they are mid-way through a modernization explicitly intended to "enable us to do a lot of things we otherwise cannot," their engineer is modernizing Clyde in public, and the Android community port already runs the real client against their official Getdown servers without a public objection ([Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/99900/discussions/0/685240596077506930/); [forum node/125065](https://forums.spiralknights.com/en/node/125065); [SKonstruct/SKapsule](https://github.com/SKonstruct/SKapsule)). What a fan team can offer them is the thing they would otherwise have to build: a TeaVM-based browser platform layer for Clyde, under BSD, that their own projectx code could be compiled against in-house. That is the Flyff Universe outcome by another route, and it is the only version of this project in which the proprietary jar and the live servers are legitimately in scope.

| Option | What the site serves | Legal exposure | Effort and durability | What you get | Dependence on Grey Havens |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. Transpile projectx via TeaVM | Grey Havens' game code, transpiled | Highest: ToS §6.1/§6.13, copyright in code, Eaglercraft and PSO Reborn precedent | Medium to first boot; resets partially with every re-obfuscated patch | Full client, useless without §6.15-violating server access | None sought; maximum antagonism |
| B. CheerpJ loads the user's own jar | Runtime and glue only | Medium: nothing of theirs distributed, but developer still reverse-engineers; untested theory | Blocked on vendor JNI toolchain and Java 21+; slow start; commercial license | Possibly a full client with no server | None sought; still antagonistic if connected |
| C. Clean-room client on open Clyde/Narya, BYO assets, own server | BSD/LGPL code and your own | Lowest available; LGPL obligations met by publishing source; residual ToS exposure for developers who play | Highest and longest; the Clyde runtime work is reusable under every option | Viewer and tools early; offline or community-server play later | Optional; the best posture for a later blessing |
| D. Blessed or official port | Whatever Grey Havens licenses | Lowest if agreed | Depends on them; your Clyde runtime is the contribution | The live game in a browser | Total |

The recommendation is **Option C as the engineering track and Option D as the relationship track, started on the same day**. Option C produces the artifact that makes D plausible and loses nothing if D never happens; it also obeys every item on the survivors' checklist, which is a name and domain free of Spiral Knights trademarks (the ToS asserts marks in the game's names), zero rights-holder code or assets served, fully open source so the "no copied expression" claim is auditable, no revenue including ads, no contact with official servers, and a self-hosted Git mirror from day one because a single GitHub notice can erase an entire fork network of hundreds or thousands of repositories ([SK ToS](https://www.spiralknights.com/tos.xhtml); [TorrentFreak](https://torrentfreak.com/nintendo-easily-kills-4238-switch-emu-repos-on-yuzu-lawsuit-anniversary-250305/)). The approach to Grey Havens should be a short, private email to the ToS and support addresses before anything public, describing the viewer-first architecture and the self-imposed server rule in the same words Knight Launcher uses, because the 2021 thread shows that a surprise public announcement is the one move guaranteed to produce the worst response ([SK forums node/124035](https://forums.spiralknights.com/en/node/124035); [lucasluqui/KnightLauncher](https://github.com/lucasluqui/KnightLauncher)).

## Phase the port: viewer, world, wire, then blessing

The proof of concept to build first is a **browser Clyde model viewer**: the player drops or picks their Spiral Knights install folder, the page reads one articulated model file and its referenced materials, textures and animations from rsrc, and renders it animating on a WebGL2 canvas with orbit controls, A/B-comparable against the desktop Clyde ModelViewer that SpiralView already wraps. This target is chosen because it forces the three highest-risk subsystems into the open at once while touching nothing proprietary. First, it compiles Clyde's export package through TeaVM, which means confronting the fact that the BinaryImporter resolves classes by name at runtime while TeaVM forbids dynamic class lookup; the solution is a build-time generated registry of every Exportable class with constructors and field setters, plus TeaVM reflection-policy entries, and an importer that skips unknown classes gracefully instead of failing, which is also what allows the viewer to tolerate game-specific config references it cannot resolve ([TeaVM overview](https://teavm.org/docs/intro/overview.html); [BinaryExporter.java](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/threerings/clyde/master/core/src/main/java/com/threerings/export/BinaryExporter.java)). Second, it forces the renderer decision: a Java facade standing in for LWJGL's GL11 through GL30 static surface, implemented over WebGL2 through TeaVM's JavaScript interop, in which fixed-function state (matrix stack, lights, fog, texgen, texture environments, alpha test, clip planes, client arrays) is tracked in Java and realized as uniforms and generated shader permutations, exactly the Eaglercraft design, while VBOs, VAOs, framebuffers and GLSL programs pass through to native WebGL2 with a shader-source translation step from desktop GLSL to GLSL ES 3.00 (version pragma, in/out qualifiers, declared fragment outputs, the overloaded texture function, precision defaults) ([Lingpand/webmc README](https://github.com/Lingpand/webmc); [webgl2fundamentals](https://webgl2fundamentals.org/webgl/lessons/webgl1-to-webgl2.html)). Third, it forces AWT out: texture decoding goes through the browser's image decoder into WebGL rather than BufferedImage, and text goes through a canvas-rasterized glyph atlas, mirroring the lesson SKapsule learned when it needed caciocavallo ([SKonstruct/SKapsule](https://github.com/SKonstruct/SKapsule)). Exit criteria are concrete: the model renders pixel-comparable to desktop at 60 FPS on an integrated GPU, both the JavaScript and Wasm GC TeaVM targets build from the same source, the Java-side platform layer has a desktop LWJGL3 twin so crashes can be debugged on the JVM, and the page never reads anything from the install's code/ directory. The viewer is also the ideal exhibit for the Grey Havens conversation, because it is useful to their modding community today and demonstrates the posture of the whole project.

| 6. Blessing | Live service in the browser | Only with Grey Havens' authorization: a gateway they operate in front of 47624, account authentication they control, their projectx compiled against the open browser runtime | Determined by Grey Havens | Unknown |

Networking and servers deserve a direct answer, because they are where fan ports of live MMOs die. The transport problem is the easy part. WebSocket preserves the ordering and reliability semantics the Presents protocol already assumes, so a gateway can forward each 4-byte-length-prefixed frame as one binary WebSocket message and vice versa, in the style of websockify or Eaglercraft's EaglerXServer, with TLS terminated at the gateway because any HTTPS page must speak wss ([novnc/websockify](https://github.com/novnc/websockify); [Sealos](https://sealos.io/blog/eaglercraft-client-server-gateway/)). The secure handshake's RSA and AES operations should go through Web Crypto via interop rather than compiled Java, since TeaVM covers 8% of java.security, and the exact cipher modes can be read from Narya's open AESAuthRequest and SecureResponse sources ([TeaVM JCL report](https://teavm.org/jcl-report/recent/jcl.html)). The UDP datagram channel is optional in the protocol's own design (it is only used if the datagram ports answer), so the browser client simply runs TCP-only, and WebTransport, which became Baseline when Safari 26.4 shipped it in March 2026, is the later candidate for an unreliable path if a server endpoint is ever added ([BlockingCommunicator.java](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/threerings/narya/master/core/src/main/java/com/threerings/presents/client/BlockingCommunicator.java); [caniuse WebTransport](https://caniuse.com/webtransport); [WebKit: Safari 26.4](https://webkit.org/blog/17862/webkit-features-in-safari-26-4/)). Because Chrome stops firing requestAnimationFrame in hidden tabs and throttles timers to roughly once per second, the keepalive and read loop must live in a worker or interval rather than the frame loop, and audible audio lifts the throttling ([Chrome: Background tabs](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/background_tabs)).

The server problem is the hard part and has three honest answers. The first is a real JVM Narya/Tudey server run locally or by the community, which Thetya demonstrates can be stood up on the open stack (TudeyServer, Presents, Guice, a SQL database) even though its game logic would have to be clean-room; this is the development and offline-play target for Phases 3 and 4 ([ThetyaServer.java](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/excal69/thetya/main/src/com/github/thetya/server/ThetyaServer.java)). The second is an in-tab server compiled through TeaVM and run in a Web Worker, which the GunZ port proved is feasible for a C++ server and which would make the offline slice installable as a self-contained PWA; Narya's server-side dependence on Guice, database persistence and real threads makes this a stretch goal rather than a baseline ([dev.to GunZ port](https://dev.to/whiplash/how-i-fully-ported-a-heavily-windows-dependent-online-game-to-the-browser-with-webassembly-1fea)). The third is a gateway operated by Grey Havens in front of their 47624 listeners under Option D. What is not an answer is pointing the fan client at the live servers without permission: that is §6.13 and §6.15, it is the act Daybreak and Blizzard are now characterizing as circumvention, and Knight Launcher's self-imposed rule exists precisely because its author was banned for less ([SK ToS](https://www.spiralknights.com/tos.xhtml); [Aftermath](https://aftermath.site/everquest-the-heroes-journey-lawsuit-update/); [lucasluqui/KnightLauncher](https://github.com/lucasluqui/KnightLauncher)).

The browser-platform decisions follow the constraints documented in 2026. WebGL2 is the first and, for a long time, only renderer target: it has been universal since Safari 15, whereas WebGPU is still unshipped in Firefox on Linux and Android and on Intel Macs and requires Android 12 with Qualcomm or ARM GPUs in Chrome, and a 2011-era forward renderer gains nothing from WGSL translation; Chrome 146's WebGPU compatibility mode converges WebGPU onto the WebGL2 device base, so keep the renderer abstracted and revisit later ([Khronos blog](https://www.khronos.org/blog/webgl-2-achieves-pervasive-support-from-all-major-web-browsers); [web.dev](https://web.dev/blog/webgpu-supported-major-browsers); [gpuweb wiki](https://github.com/gpuweb/gpuweb/wiki/Implementation-Status); [Chrome Developers](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/new-in-webgpu-146)). The baseline build is single-threaded with no cross-origin isolation requirement, following Godot's reversal, with TeaVM's green threads carrying the loader and network "threads" and CPU-heavy decoding moved to Web Workers; JSPI, now in Chrome 137 and Safari 27 with Firefox 153 scheduled, is an opportunistic enhancement for the Wasm GC build, not a dependency ([Godot 4.3 progress report](https://godotengine.org/article/progress-report-web-export-in-4-3/); [features.json](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/WebAssembly/website/main/features.json); [WebKit: Safari 27 beta](https://webkit.org/blog/17967/news-from-wwdc26-webkit-in-safari-27-beta/)). Memory planning assumes a working set well under 1 GB on desktop and far less on mobile Safari, where the tab is killed under pressure with no catchable out-of-memory and repeated reloads shrink available memory, so zone assets must be paged in from OPFS and released on area transitions rather than held ([WebKit bug 221530](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=221530); [WebKit bug 269777](https://www2.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=269777)). Storage uses OPFS with synchronous access handles in a worker as the content store, treated as a rebuildable cache validated against a manifest at start, because Safari evicts script-created data after seven days without interaction for non-installed sites and quota eviction is silent and all-or-nothing per origin; the mitigations are a persistent-storage request, a PWA install prompt, and the Chromium-only directory-picker fast path to re-hydrate from the user's install, with drag-and-drop as the Firefox and Safari fallback ([MDN: Storage quotas](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Storage_API/Storage_quotas_and_eviction_criteria); [web.dev: Storage for the web](https://web.dev/articles/storage-for-the-web); [Chrome Developers: File System Access](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/capabilities/web-apis/file-system-access)). Audio keeps the OpenAL call shape and gates the whole client behind a Play click so the AudioContext, Pointer Lock and fullscreen all start inside a user gesture ([Chrome Developers: Autoplay](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/autoplay); [MDN Pointer Lock API](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Pointer_Lock_API)). Hosting is a dedicated HTTPS origin, because that is the only configuration supporting isolation headers, Brotli precompression, range requests, multi-gigabyte storage and PWA installability at once; itch.io's 500 MB cap rules it out for anything beyond a bootstrap ([itch.io HTML5 docs](https://itch.io/docs/creators/html5); [Cloudflare Pages headers](https://developers.cloudflare.com/pages/configuration/headers/)).

| Risk | Why it is real | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Fixed-function emulation is wrong or slow | Clyde's renderer leans on matrix stack, lighting, fog, texgen, texture environments and client arrays that WebGL2 lacks | Eaglercraft-style state tracker with cached shader permutations; pixel A/B against desktop Clyde from Phase 1; progressively convert Clyde materials to explicit shaders so the emulator shrinks |
| Closed-world compiler versus reflective engine | Clyde's export, config and editor systems resolve classes by name; TeaVM restricts dynamic lookup and whitelists reflection | Build-time generated class registry; exclude the Swing editor packages entirely; tolerant importer that skips unknown classes |
| AWT dependency surfaces unexpectedly | SKapsule needed a headless AWT bridge; fonts, images, Toolkit calls are scattered | Audit with the desktop twin; canvas glyph atlas; browser image decode; DOM overlays for dialogs and chat input |
| Green threads stall rendering | Background decode or synchronization on a TeaVM "thread" blocks the single JS thread; suspending inside the frame callback throws | Frame callback never suspends; decoders and extraction in Web Workers; JSPI-enabled Wasm GC build as an enhancement |
| TeaVM Wasm GC codegen defects | Fresh miscompile bugs filed against 0.16.0 | Keep the JavaScript backend as the reference build; both targets from one source |
| Memory ceilings, especially Safari | Tabs killed silently under pressure; reloads shrink available memory | Stream and evict zone assets; conservative initial heap; mobile Safari out of scope until Phase 5 |
| Storage eviction and quota | Seven-day eviction on Safari; silent LRU eviction | Manifest validation on start; persistent-storage request; PWA install; re-hydrate from the user's folder |
| Game-catalog schemas drift | Catalog objects are projectx classes, re-obfuscated per patch | Pin to a game version via version.txt; clean-room schemas keyed on observed field names, which do not change when class names do |
| Content availability offline | Scenes are server-streamed and only cached when visited | Target player-made Crucible arenas and a single dungeon floor; never redistribute cached scenes |
| Legal action from Grey Havens | Live service, explicit ToS, 2021 precedent, PSO Reborn | No projectx bytes served; no live-server connection; non-trademark name; no revenue; early private contact; self-hosted mirror |
| LGPL §6 on whole-program bundles | Unresolved for transpiled output | Publish the entire client source under open licenses |
| Debuggability and build time | Browser-side debugging of TeaVM output is JS-only in IDEA; Wasm GC relies on a stack deobfuscator | Desktop LWJGL3 twin as the primary debug harness, as Eaglercraft does |

## Conclusion

The question began as "how do I port a Java game to the browser," and the research answers that the compiler is the least of it. TeaVM has quietly become mature enough, with Wasm GC, coroutine threads, Java 25 support and a shipping libGDX backend, that the Eaglercraft architecture is reproducible by a small team, and Clyde's own 2026 move to Java 25 and LWJGL 3 means the open engine is closer to a web platform layer than it has ever been. What makes Spiral Knights different from every C/C++ port in the current wave is that the community never wrote or decompiled the game layer: the engine is open but the game is a sealed, shifting jar, and the game is alive. That inverts the usual bring-your-own-files logic. For Diablo or Ocarina of Time the player supplies data to a community engine; for Spiral Knights the player can supply data to a community-ported engine, but the rules that turn data into a game remain behind a Terms of Service that a live, monetized, actively developed service will enforce.

The implication is that the project is really two projects wearing one name. The first, a browser runtime for Clyde under BSD with a WebGL2 fixed-function emulator, a WebSocket Presents transport and an OPFS asset store, is unambiguously buildable, legally clean, useful to modders from its first milestone, and valuable to Grey Havens whether or not they ever acknowledge it. The second, Spiral Knights itself playable in a tab against live servers, is not a technical problem at all; it is a relationship with a six-person founding team that has just spent a year modernizing the client "to enable things we otherwise cannot" and has said in its own words that it works with people who build tools and honor its rights. Build the first project as if the second will never happen, and offer it in a way that makes the second possible.
